Stress & Rhythm

Indonesian has no tones. Stress is light and usually falls on the second-to-last syllable, e.g. se‑KO‑lah (school). Every letter is pronounced — there are no silent letters except a soft final "h".

Numbers — Angka
Word Order: Subject + Verb + Object

Same basic order as English, so building simple sentences is intuitive.

Saya makan nasi.
I eat rice
I eat rice.
No Verb Conjugation

Verbs never change for person or number — one form covers "I eat", "she eats", "they eat", etc.

Saya makan. / Dia makan. / Mereka makan.
I eat. / He or she eats. / They eat.
Tense Comes From Words, Not Endings

There's no past/present/future conjugation either — add a time word instead: sudah (already), sedang (currently), akan (will), belum (not yet).

Saya sudah makan.
I already ate.
Saya sedang makan.
I am eating.
Saya akan makan.
I will eat.
Plurals by Reduplication

Repeat the noun to make it plural, or just pair it with a number — the noun itself never changes.

buku → buku-buku / dua buku
book → books / two books
Adjectives Come After the Noun

The opposite order from English.

rumah besar
house big
big house
No Articles

There's no word for "a", "an", or "the" — context tells you which is meant.

Saya punya buku.
I have a book. / I have the book.
