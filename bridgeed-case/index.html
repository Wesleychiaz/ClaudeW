BridgeED

The “Third Pillar” of Singapore’s EdTech — connecting the home, the school & the student.

An automated parental-reinforcement platform that syncs with the Student Learning Space (SLS) and turns each week’s lessons into a 1-minute action a parent can actually take — at near-zero cost to the teacher.

01 The gap

There’s an information blackout between the classroom and the home.

SLS gives students a world-class place to learn. Parents Gateway handles admin. But no one serves the parent as a learning partner — so families can’t see what’s being taught, or how to help.

60%
of parents feel “blind” to their child’s day-to-day school learning.
10+hrs
a week teachers spend on manual parent updates across WhatsApp, ClassDojo, SLS & PG.

And MOE’s own AI roadmap reinforces the gap. The AI features now in SLS — Adaptive Learning, Teaching Assistants, Feedback Assistants — map to MOE’s “AI in Education” framework, which has exactly three levels. None of them is the home.

02 Who hurts

A two-sided problem.

The same gap frustrates the parent who wants to help and overloads the teacher who can’t add more work.

Parent · “Priya”

The concerned parent

“I feel like a stranger to my son’s education. I ask how school was — he says ‘ok lor’. Then his results come back poor.”
  • The “I don’t know / ok” barrier when asking about school
  • Finds out the child has struggled for weeks only at test time
  • Unsure how to support meaningfully — or what was even covered
Teacher · “Tan”

The overburdened teacher

“I want to focus on teaching — not spend my evenings updating anxious parents across five different apps.”
  • 10+ hrs/week on manual reporting → “app fatigue” & burnout
  • Constant “noisy” communication and parent pressure
  • Manual parent-comms is simply unsustainable at class scale
03 The insight

Deliver parent value at near-zero teacher cost.

BridgeED is the “Third Pillar” of the MOE EdTech stack. The wedge: it rides on data that already exists in SLS and uses AI to translate it — so parents get structured, bite-sized actions without adding to the teacher’s load.

Product vision: an integrated education ecosystem where schools and families work in harmony to nurture every child’s potential — replacing “noisy” communication with structured learning.

04 The product

One weekly loop. Four screens.

A parent absorbs the week in a minute; a teacher publishes it in under five. The loop is the product.

1. This Week→ 2. Snapshot→ 3. Playbook→ 4. Progress
This Week — what your child learned, in one glance
Snapshot — the 1-minute curriculum summary
Playbook — a 5-min real-world activity (e.g. fractions over “Pizza Night”)
Progress — streaks & concepts covered, “am I supporting my child?”
Curriculum Snapshots
Complex syllabus goals → 1-minute, jargon-free summaries for busy parents.
Micro-Reinforcement Playbook
Low-effort, high-impact home activities tied to this week’s lesson.
Teacher: review & push
Direct SLS sync auto-generates snapshots — teacher just approves. <5 min/week.

07 The bet & the risks

Start small. Earn the right to scale.

A focused pilot to validate the dual-impact model before going wide — and an honest read on what could break it.

The MVP bet

12-week pilot · Primary 3 Math5 schools · 10 teachers · ~300 students & parents.
Why P3?The anxiety inflection point — Science enters, math gets harder, GEP transition. Families need alignment most here.
Why a SAP + neighbourhood mix?To prove BridgeED works as a social leveler, not just for the already-advantaged.
Then: Pilot → Growth (30 schools) → Scale (all 180 primary schools).

Risks I’d address head-on

“Won’t MOE just build this?”BridgeED proves the parent-reinforcement model and is designed to plug into SLS — as a pilot, partner, or pattern MOE adopts.
AI accuracySnapshots must be pedagogically correct → human-in-the-loop: teachers rate & approve every output.
Parent drop-off“Time-poverty” kills habit apps → catch-up playbooks + light gamification to lower the barrier.
SustainabilityTCOO cost-recovery (~$1.50/student/mo) → self-funding by Year 2, surplus to an R&D sinking fund.

BridgeED is the missing link — turning ‘home’ from a source of anxiety into a second site for learning.
